## 1. The Herzliya Sanctuary | בית ההורים בהרצליה

Tamar "Tami" Sheaffer was born on October 25, 1956, and grew up in the coastal town of Herzliya during the formative early decades of the State of Israel. Her childhood home was characterized by deep warmth, historical gravity, and constant activity[^seed-herzliya].

Her parents, **Michael Fried** and **Ahuva Fried (née Lubetkin)**, represented the quiet heroism of the generation that survived the Holocaust and built the country with their bare hands—a couple fondly remembered as *"מלח הארץ"* (salt of the earth)[^seed-herzliya].

### Ahuva's Kindergarten & Warmth
Ahuva, who had survived separation from her European family when arriving in Mandatory Palestine via Aliyat Hanoar in 1938, was endowed with an extraordinary gift for children[^blog-ahuva]. She operated a beloved kindergarten directly in the large, leafy yard of their Herzliya house. Young Tami grew up with the sounds of children singing, playing in sandboxes, and learning under her mother's patient, radiant guidance. This kindergarten atmosphere imbued Tami with a lifelong appreciation for play, creative freedom, and educational nurturing.

### Michael's "Hands of Gold" & Protective Vigilance
Her father Michael, who had arrived on the shores of Tel Aviv in August 1939 aboard the illegal immigrant ship *Parita*, was a man of manual genius[^blog-parita]. Known to all as possessing "hands of gold" (ידי זהב), Michael had a small workshop on the property where he repaired tools, fashioned wooden furniture, and maintained every inch of the home with meticulous precision[^seed-herzliya].

Alongside his craftsmanship, Michael had two distinct traits that left an indelible mark on family lore:
1. **Safety Worries:** He was the eternal, caring worrier—always inspecting window latches, double-checking door locks, and fretting over the safety and health of his daughters[^seed-herzliya].
2. **Television Ritual:** He was a passionate, unyielding viewer of the daily soap opera *The Bold and the Beautiful* (*היפים והאמיצים*), organizing his afternoon schedule around its melodramatic twists[^seed-herzliya].

## 2. Sisterly Bond with Er'ela | הקשר עם אראלה

Growing up alongside her sister **Er'ela**, Tami experienced a bond of profound closeness and endless hilarity. The two sisters shared a secret language of wry humor, observing the world with sharp irony[^seed-erela]. 

Even as adults living in different cities, Tami and Er'ela would speak by phone almost daily. Family members recall how these calls were punctuated by unstoppable, ringing laughter that echoed through the hallways[^seed-erela]. Er'ela also possessed a distinct creative and aesthetic sensibility, making it clear that artistic expression was a shared family trait.

*Read more: [Sister: Er'ela Fried](../family/erela-fried.md).*

## 3. The Artistic Spark & The Pragmatic Choice | בין אמנות למעשיות

From a very young age, Tami displayed an intense love for visual art, drawing, and spatial construction. She would sketch with obsessive detail, fascinated by architectural blueprints, maps, and physical textures. 

However, coming of age in the 1970s—in a young country still recovering from the Yom Kippur War and deeply focused on national defense, economic modernization, and scientific rigor—art was regarded by many as an impractical luxury. Tami made the mature, pragmatic choice: she decided to channel her formidable intellect and spatial problem-solving skills into science and technology.

## 4. The Technion Years & Meeting Gadi | שנות הטכניון ומפגש החיים

In 1974, Tami moved to Haifa to study at the country's most demanding engineering institution: the **Technion — Israel Institute of Technology**. She enrolled in the Department of Computer Science, embarking on a rigorous B.Sc. curriculum centered on algorithmic theory, real-time systems, and software engineering.

The Technion environment was demanding, competitive, and overwhelmingly male. Tami excelled, developing a disciplined methodology for managing complex systems that would serve her throughout her professional career.

Crucially, it was during these student years on Mount Carmel that Tami met **Gad "Gadi" Sheaffer**, a Haifa native (born 1954) studying engineering[^blog-gadi]. Their meeting marked the beginning of a lifelong partnership based on mutual respect, intellectual dialogue, and shared adventures. They married in the late 1970s and made Haifa their permanent home.
