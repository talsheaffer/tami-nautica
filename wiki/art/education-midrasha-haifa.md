# Art Education: HaMidrasha & Haifa University MFA
# לימודי אמנות: המדרשה ותואר שני באוניברסיטת חיפה

Tami Sheaffer's formal entry into the Israeli art world represents a rare and courageous mid-life metamorphosis. Having reached senior engineering standing and contributed decades of systemic leadership to Elbit Systems and Rafael Advanced Defense Systems, she chose early retirement to pursue full-time academic art training, studying sequentially at two of Israel's premier institutions: **HaMidrasha School of Art (2005–2009)** and the **University of Haifa (MFA, 2009–2011)**[^site-about].

---

## The HaMidrasha Years (2005–2009)

In 2005, Tami enrolled at the celebrated **Faculty of Arts – HaMidrasha at Beit Berl College (המדרשה לאמנות — מכללת בית ברל)**. HaMidrasha was historically renowned as the epicenter of Israeli conceptualism, radical critical discourse, and interdisciplinary experimentation, led by prominent figures in Israeli contemporary art.

Entering HaMidrasha in her late forties alongside students half her age, Tami brought a unique intellectual maturity:
- **Systemic Rigor meets Conceptual Subversion:** Where younger students often struggled with discipline, Tami possessed decades of mastery in project planning, structural analysis, and technical drawing. What HaMidrasha gave her was the conceptual permission to subvert that very discipline—to dismantle rigid structures, embrace intentional absurdity, and question the ideological foundations of institutional systems.
- **Mastery of Diverse Media:** During her four years at Beit Berl, Tami moved rapidly beyond traditional painting, experimenting with sculpture, performance, site-specific interventions, and printmaking.
- **Graduation (2009):** She completed her studies with distinction in 2009, immediately presenting in the acclaimed duo exhibition *"Connections"* at the Municipal Gallery of Kfar Saba (curator: Revital Segev)[^site-about].

---

## The MFA at University of Haifa (2009–2011)

Refusing to rest on her undergraduate achievements, Tami immediately pursued a Master of Fine Arts (MFA) degree in the Department of Fine Arts at the **University of Haifa (2009–2011)**[^site-about]. Situated atop Mount Carmel, the Haifa MFA program is known for its rigorous studio-critique culture and theoretical depth.

During her MFA, Tami crystallized the core physical and conceptual elements of her mature style:
1. **The Introduction of Earth and Hamra:** Moving beyond conventional sculptural armatures, Tami began incorporating raw earth, compressed sand, and soil mixed with binders.
2. **The "Code" (צופן) Project:** For her final MFA research and subsequent exhibition at the Tel Aviv Museum of Art (2011/2012, *"8-Cube"*, curated by Nili Goren z"l, Sally Haftel Naveh, and Nogah Davidson), Tami created 200 handmade bricks cast from compressed red hamra earth, manually stamped with geometric patterns resembling QR codes arranged across tables[^binyamin-code]. The work examined the appropriation and militarization of land alongside the digital surveillance of public space.
3. **Synthesis with Architectural Drafting:** She developed her signature hybrid drafting technique, combining freehand graphite sketching with precise technical drafting using rulers, compasses, and plastic stencils.

---

## Legacy of Her Dual Training

Tami's dual background—a Technion B.Sc. in Computer Science followed by HaMidrasha and a Haifa University MFA—created a singular artistic voice:
- She does not romanticize technology; having built defense systems, she is acutely aware of their blind spots, fragility, and capacity for alienation.
- Her art employs the visual language of structural engineering precisely to expose how fragile human structures and political regimes truly are.
