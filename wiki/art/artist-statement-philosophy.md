# Artist Statement & Philosophy
# הצהרת אמן ותפיסת עולם

Tami Sheaffer's artistic philosophy occupies a singular, evocative position in Israeli contemporary art. Grounded in decades of systems engineering and forged through rigorous sculptural practice, her work explores the precarious relationship between human beings, their physical shelters, and an increasingly destabilized sociopolitical reality[^site-statement].

---

## The Primary Artist Statement

In her official artist statement, Tami defines the recurring conceptual and visual engines of her work[^site-statement]:

> *"I find myself returning to the same imagery over and over again. Imaginary architectures which lack any structural logic are one central component. I see them as 'mental environments' focusing on my personal perception of my living environment.*
>
> *Another recurring theme in my works are hybrid creatures / entities that have alien, machine or animal features.*
>
> *The last element, one that accompanies me for many years, long before COVID19, is the 'capsule' – a section of enclosed space and time. Capsules interest me as a way to disconnect and isolate a small space from the noisy environment, the one expressed in my architectural landscapes.*
>
> *The viewer's movement relative to the installation is of special importance to me. At any moment, depending on the viewer's position in the space, his perception of the work changes. There is no one 'right' appearance to the installation. This, for me, is a way to metaphorically express the idea, that one's position in the world (worldview, cultural background, socioeconomic status etc.) determines his perception of reality. Reality is not one, I guess, it is viewpoint dependent.*
>
> *There is a strong connection between my installations and my drawings. Most of the time I draw with pencils but I also use ink, charcoal and watercolor. The drawings are partly done free hand and partly with rulers and stencils. The attempt to create beauty and meaning with what appears to be a technical sketch is of particular interest to me. I draw on paper, wood and wall.*
>
> *My main work techniques are deliberately low-tech, manual and Sisyphean, hoping to preserve material and sensory experience in the digital age."*

---

## Key Conceptual Tenets

### 1. Viewpoint-Dependent Reality (המציאות תלוית נקודת מבט)
Tami rejects the notion of an objective, singular, monolithic reality. Just as in Einsteinian relativity or perspective geometry, what a viewer experiences inside her installations depends strictly on their physical coordinates in space. As the spectator steps left, ducks under a pipe, or peers through a glass aperture, elements align, fracture, or disappear entirely. For Tami, this optical phenomenon is deeply political and human: it asserts that our worldview, privileges, and emotional baggage dictate what we perceive as "truth."

### 2. Imaginary Architectures & Mental Environments (אדריכלות מדומינת וסביבות תודעה)
Her installations frequently resemble buildings, rooms, conduits, or urban fragments that have undergone mutations. Walls do not support ceilings; pipes curve back into themselves; doors open onto blind partitions. These are "mental architectures"—diagrams of anxiety, psychic defense mechanisms, and existential vulnerability rendered into three dimensions.

### 3. The Low-Tech Sisyphean Ethos (אומנות ידנית וסיזיפית בעידן דיגיטלי)
In an era dominated by sleek computer-generated rendering, 3D printing, and digital screens, Tami insists on arduous, manual labor: tearing packing paper by hand, kneading paper pulp, packing cement into wooden molds with a trowel, and drafting hundreds of graphite parallel lines with a plastic stencil. This "Sisyphean" labor is an ethical stance: it preserves tactile, visceral human presence against automated alienation.

### 4. Stand-ins, Simulacra & The Vanishing "Home" (סימולקרה של הבית)
Tami's inner life is profoundly attuned to the existential precarity of Israeli society—the erosion of democratic norms, political corruption, and the omnipresent threat of violence[^user-seed]. Her art responds not through literal protest banners, but through architectural simulacra: creating "mock-ups" of domestic security (a fake closet, a padded wall, an enclosed capsule, a toy-like survival kit) that highlight just how defenseless our private sanctuaries truly are.
