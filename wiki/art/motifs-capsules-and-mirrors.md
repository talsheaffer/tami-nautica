# Motifs: Capsules, Enclosures & Distorted Mirrors
# מוטיבים: קפסולות, מארזים והשתקפויות מטושטשות

Across two decades of sculptural production, Tami Sheaffer has established a cohesive iconography of spatial and optical motifs. Prominent among these are **the capsule**, **the conduit pipe network**, and **the distorted or broken mirror**[^user-seed].

---

## 1. The Capsule: An Enclosure of Space & Time

Long before the COVID-19 pandemic made "capsules" a ubiquitous global concept, Tami was deeply preoccupied with the capsule as a sculptural and psychological unit[^site-statement].

### Characteristics of Tami's Capsules:
- **Spatial Isolation:** The capsule represents a sealed section of physical space carved out from a hostile, noisy, or chaotic exterior environment.
- **Organic-Architectural Hybrids:** Tami's capsules rarely look like sterile sci-fi pods. Instead, they merge organic cocoon shapes (wasp nests, pupae) with architectural elements (windows, vents, rivets, hatches).
- **The Herzliya Origin:** Rooted in the memory of her childhood treehouse in her parents' Herzliya yard, the capsule embodies the universal human desire to pull up the ladder, retreat inside, and be safe from the world[^binyamin-capsule].
- **Key Iterations:** *Capsule* (solo series, 2017); *It Might Be Better There* (Paper Biennale, 2017); *Survival Kit* (ערכת הישרדות, 2023).

---

## 2. Pipes, Conduits & Flawed Circulation

In works such as *"Plan B"* (Alfred Institute, 2018, curated by Ravit Harari), Tami transformed the entire gallery into an intricate labyrinth of industrial pipes, cardboard conduits, and cement fittings snaking across walls and floors[^binyamin-planb].

### Symbolic Function:
- **Passageways to Alternative Worlds:** Drawing inspiration from Etgar Keret's short story *"Pipes"* (צינורות)—where a troubled worker builds a pipe network leading out of this world into a better, kinder reality—Tami's pipes offer hypothetical escape routes from political and personal dread.
- **Blind Terminations:** Crucially, most of the pipe openings in her installations are capped with heavy concrete or metal plugs. Only select apertures remain open, allowing the viewer to bend down and peer inside into miniature illuminated dioramas—glimpses of a phantom reality that cannot be entered.
- **Acoustic Leakage:** In *Plan B*, the pipe network emitted the recorded sound of a beach matkot (paddleball) game—the quintessential Israeli seaside sound echoing uncannily through sealed industrial tubes.

---

## 3. Broken, Scratched & Blurry Mirrors

Mirrors (מראות) appear frequently throughout Tami's installations, but they never offer flattering, clear, or narcissistic reflections[^user-seed]. The recurring motifs of broken and blurry mirrors (מראות שבורות ומטושטשות) reflect a fractured domestic and national psyche.

### Optical Techniques:
- **Clouded & Distorted Surfaces:** Tami employs curved acrylic mirrors, glass plates coated in mineral dust or grease, and scratched reflective vinyl.
- **Shattered & Reassembled Panes:** In works exploring domestic interiors (*"מרחב לא מוגן"*, *"בנה ביתך"*), mirrors are broken along fracture lines, dislocating the viewer's face and body as they look into them.
- **Philosophical Import:** The broken mirror serves as an optical metaphor for the dissolution of the ego, the fractured national psyche, and the impossibility of seeing oneself clearly in a time of collective trauma.
