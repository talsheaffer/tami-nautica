# Parents: Ahuva & Michael Fried

## 1. Ahuva Fried (née Lubetkin) | אהובה פריד לבית לובטקין

Ahuva Fried was born on **October 22, 1923**, in the town of Byten (then Poland, now Belarus), into the prominent Lubetkin family[^blog-ahuva]. Her parents were Shimon and Rochel Lubetkin. Ahuva was the younger sister of **Zivia Lubetkin (צביה לובטקין)**, who would rise to legendary status as one of the leaders of the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising, a commander in the Jewish Fighting Organization (ŻOB), and a founder of Kibbutz Lohamei HaGeta'ot.

```
             +---------------------------+---------------------------+

    [ ZIVIA LUBETKIN ]                                      [ AHUVA LUBETKIN (FRIED) ]
    - Leader of Warsaw Ghetto Uprising                     - Born 22 October 1923
    - Commander in ŻOB                                     - Aliyat Hanoar to Ben Shemen (1938)
    - Founder of Kibbutz Lohamei HaGeta'ot                 - Kibbutz Mishmarot (1940)
    - National icon of resistance                          - Herzliya kindergarten teacher (d. 2006)
```

In 1938, on the eve of the Holocaust, 15-year-old Ahuva made Aliyah to Mandatory Palestine via **Aliyat Hanoar**, leaving her parents behind[^blog-ahuva]. She studied at the Ben Shemen Youth Village, and in 1940 joined **Kibbutz Mishmarot**, where she worked in agriculture and early childhood care. Her parents and many relatives perished in the Shoah, making her survival and subsequent family-building in Israel a testament to quiet, determined renewal.

In Herzliya, Ahuva established a renowned private kindergarten in the garden of their home. She was known for her extraordinary emotional warmth, her musical storytelling, and her instinctive understanding of young children[^seed-parents]. Ahuva passed away on **June 8, 2006**, at the age of 82. Her husband Michael did not survive even a year after her passing, dying less than six months later on November 26, 2006.

## 2. Michael Fried | מיכאל פריד

Michael Fried was a true *"מלח הארץ"* (salt of the earth)[^seed-parents]. In August 1939, just days before the outbreak of World War II, Michael arrived in Mandatory Palestine aboard the famous Betar illegal immigration ship **"פאריטה" (*Parita*)**[^blog-parita]. 

The *Parita* had spent 42 harrowing days at sea under catastrophic conditions of starvation, disease, and naval blockades. On August 22, 1939, its captain ran the vessel aground deliberately directly on the Tel Aviv beach (opposite the current Bograshov/Frishman beach), allowing hundreds of exhausted refugees to scramble ashore into the waiting arms of Tel Aviv citizens before British troops could intervene[^blog-parita].

### "Hands of Gold" (ידי זהב)
Michael possessed exceptional mechanical and carpentry skills. In his home workshop, he could construct, repair, or restore almost anything. Neighbors, friends, and family constantly sought his help. He passed on this tactile relationship with materials—wood, screws, metal, and structural integrity—to his daughter Tami, whose later installation art reflects a deep curiosity about physical construction[^seed-parents].

### The Caring Worrier & *The Bold and the Beautiful*
Two personal traits defined Michael in family affection:
1. **The Safety Worrier:** He was perpetually concerned about security and physical protection. He meticulously checked doors, latches, locks, and electrical cords, worrying constantly about the well-being of Ahuva, Tami, and Er'ela[^seed-parents].
2. **Soap Opera Devotee:** Every afternoon, Michael's workshop came to a standstill as he watched *The Bold and the Beautiful* (*היפים והאמיצים*), deeply invested in the ongoing sagas of the Forrester and Spectra families[^seed-parents].

Born Paul ("Poli") Fried on May 13, 1920 in Satu Mare, Romania, Michael passed away on November 26, 2006, leaving behind a legacy of absolute devotion, honesty, and manual artistry.

## 3. Heritage in Tami's Life & Art | מורשת ההורים בחייה וביצירתה של תמי

Tami is the living synthesis of her parents:
- From **Ahuva**, she inherited warmth, emotional resilience, educational patience, and delight in the play of her granddaughters.
- From **Michael**, she inherited manual courage, an instinct for tools and raw materials, and the vigilant "architecture of worry" that protects loved ones and animates her art.
