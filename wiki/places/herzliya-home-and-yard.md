# Herzliya: Parents' Home & Kindergarten Yard
# בית ההורים והחצר בהרצליה

The family home in Herzliya where Tamar "Tami" Sheaffer was born and raised stands as the deep emotional bedrock of her life. Owned and cultivated by her parents, **Michael Fried** and **Ahuva Fried (née Lubetkin)**, the house and its expansive, tree-shaded yard embodied the essence of *"מלח הארץ"* (salt of the earth)—a warm, unpretentious, deeply welcoming sanctuary grounded in labor, family love, and community care[^user-seed].

---

## The House & The Kindergarten Yard

The property on the central coastal plain was not merely a private residence; it was a vibrant communal haven. Ahuva, renowned for her extraordinary gentleness, empathy, and intuitive understanding of childhood psychology, ran a neighborhood kindergarten in the yard[^blog-ahuva]. Decades of local children grew up under the shade of the fruit trees, playing in sandboxes and climbing wooden play structures built by Michael.

Michael, a man blessed with legendary *"ידי זהב"* (hands of gold), maintained an impeccably organized workshop in the yard. Constantly building, repairing, and safety-proofing the domestic perimeter, Michael was an instinctive craftsman whose practical woodworking and mechanical skill fascinated young Tami. Yet alongside his technical ingenuity ran a profound streak of tender worry—he was the ever-vigilant protector, acutely attuned to physical hazards and family safety, who famously spent his quiet evenings engrossed in episodes of *The Bold and the Beautiful* (היפים והאמיצים)[^user-seed].

---

## The Treehouse: Seed of the Artistic Capsule

Among the citrus and deciduous trees of the Herzliya yard, young Tami built a small treehouse perched high in the foliage. Accessible only by climbing branches and hidden from the sight of adults and kindergarteners below, this wooden platform became her private universe—a space of profound peace, daydreaming, and creative contemplation.

Decades later, when exhibiting at the 3rd Israeli Paper Biennale at the Eretz Israel Museum (2017), Tami explicitly identified this childhood treehouse as the direct conceptual origin of her signature sculptural capsules[^binyamin-capsule]:

<div dir="rtl" lang="he">
> "שתי חוויות מאוד שונות היו השראה לעבודה זו: סיפורו של בקט 'האבודים', ו'בית' שהיה לי בילדותי על עץ בחצר והיה מקום מפלט מושלם. הקפסולות עשויות נייר אריזה חום פשוט והן חלולות... מקום המיועד למנוחה וכמפלט מהכאוס שמסביב."
</div>

> *"Two very different experiences inspired this work: Samuel Beckett's story 'The Lost Ones', and a 'house' I had during my childhood on a tree in the yard that served as a perfect place of refuge. The capsules are made of simple brown packing paper and are hollow... a place intended for rest and as a sanctuary from the surrounding chaos."*

This visceral memory of shelter amidst nature became the template for Tami's enduring artistic exploration of enclosures, cocoons, and protective mental environments.

---

## Sisterhood & Memory

The Herzliya house was also the shared realm of Tami and her beloved sister, **Er'ela Fried**[^user-seed]. Growing up together in the yard, Tami and Er'ela formed an unbreakable, lifelong bond characterized by uncontrollable phone laughter, sharp humor, and a shared artistic sensibility. Long after both had established their own adult homes, memories of their Herzliya youth remained their shared touchstone.

Following Ahuva's passing on June 8, 2006 (at age 82) and Michael's passing less than six months later on November 26, 2006, the physical house passed into memory, but its architectural and psychological imprint remains indelibly alive in Tami's daily life and artistic practice.
