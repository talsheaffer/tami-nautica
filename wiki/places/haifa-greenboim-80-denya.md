# Haifa: 80 Greenboim St., Denya (Home & Studio)
# גרינבוים 80, דניה (הבית והסטודיו)

Since **2004**, the family residence and Tami Sheaffer's principal artistic studio have been located at **80 Greenboim Street** in the Hod HaCarmel (Denya) neighborhood of Haifa. Nestled on the southern slopes of Mount Carmel, this custom-designed, modernist residence is both a serene domestic retreat and the primary physical engine behind Tami's major contemporary installations[^blog-g80].

---

## Architectural Concept & Topography

The creation of 80 Greenboim St. was a multi-year labor of architectural passion. Rather than purchasing a conventional developer home, Tami and Gadi acquired an unusual, challenging parcel of land: a triangular lot of slightly over 400 square meters situated in a steep stream pocket (אפיק נחל) bordering an untouched Mediterranean wadi[^blog-g80]. The lot was subject to a strict 32% building footprint restriction and was attached on one side to a pre-existing semi-detached neighbor.

While initial architects advised selling the awkward, sloping plot, Tami and Gadi embraced its topological drama. Drawing inspiration from mid-century modern masters (Mies van der Rohe, Marcel Breuer, and Le Corbusier), they designed a home that harmonized with the rugged terrain:
- **Pillars & Natural Ventilation:** Remembering the blissful cooling currents of their Einstein apartment, they elevated the structure on concrete pilotis, allowing cool mountain breezes to sweep underneath the house and naturally cool the living quarters[^blog-g80].
- **Intimate Forest Panorama:** Eschewing distant sea views that required perched, detached terraces, the house faces inward toward the lush green foliage of the wadi, immersing the interior in the rhythms of Carmel oak, pine, and wild fauna.
- **Architectural Acclaim:** The design and construction of the house attracted national architectural attention, culminating in a dedicated feature article by esteemed architecture critic **Esther Zandberg in *Haaretz***[^haaretz-zandberg].

---

## Tami's Studio: The Tactile Crucible

The ground level of 80 Greenboim houses Tami's private art studio. Serving as both intellectual sanctuary and heavy physical workshop, the studio is where Tami transitions seamlessly between precise technical sketching and messy, Sisyphean manual labor:
- **Paper Pulp & Mâché Stations:** Vats for soaking packaging paper, shredding pulp, and sculpting delicate paper skins over wire armatures.
- **Casting & Heavy Assembly:** Areas dedicated to mixing sand, soil, and industrial concrete, casting structural bricks, and assembling heavy conduits and metal pipes.
- **Drafting Tables:** Large architectural worktables equipped with drafting rulers, stencils, and graphite pencils where she renders her intricate, paradoxically illogical blueprints.

The studio opens directly toward the wadi garden, allowing sculptures to dry in the open Mediterranean air and creating a continuous dialogue between raw wilderness and industrial cast forms. Adjacent to Tami's studio sits Gadi's woodworking shop, establishing the home as a shared sanctuary of craftsmanship and creative dedication.

---

## Domesticity vs. Disintegration: The Home as Subject

Paradoxically, while 80 Greenboim represents a painstakingly constructed sanctuary of domestic security, it is also the site where Tami developed her most searing critiques of domestic vulnerability. In this very studio, she conceived and built the fractured, sawed, and reconstructed furniture pieces for her landmark 2026 solo exhibition **"בנה ביתך / The New Normal"** at the Ramat Gan Museum of Israeli Art[^rgma-bene-beitcha]. The tension between her secure Carmel home and the crumbling sense of collective national safety forms the core engine of her mature art.
