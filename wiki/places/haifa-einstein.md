# Haifa: 33A Einstein St., Ahuza
# רחוב איינשטיין 33א, אחוזה, חיפה

From **1992 to 2004**, the family home was situated at **33A Einstein Street** in the Ahuza neighborhood of Haifa. Occupying the high ridge of Mount Carmel, this quiet, green residence was the place where younger son **Tal Sheaffer** was born and where both boys grew from toddlers into young men[^blog-einstein].

---

## Setting & Architectural Layout

Departing Hadar in search of tranquility, Tami and Gadi purchased the left-hand apartment on the first floor above the open stilt pillars (קומת עמודים מפולשת) in a quiet rear building (בית אחורי) at 33A Einstein St. Descending about twenty gentle steps from the street, the building projected directly into a lush, secluded public park nestled between Einstein and Hantke streets, just three minutes' walk from Horev Center[^blog-einstein].

The apartment featured:
- Three bedrooms arranged linearly along a central hallway.
- A massive north-facing living room window framing panoramic views across Haifa Bay and the mountains of the Galilee (eventually sheltered by the growing canopy of park treetops).
- An exceptionally cool summer microclimate generated by the Carmel ridge elevation and the natural drafts circulating beneath the open stilt pillars—a thermal phenomenon so pleasant that the couple later incorporated elevated stilt architecture into the home they built in Denya[^blog-einstein].

To adapt the standard apartment to their taste, they removed the upper partition between the kitchen and living room and merged the master bedroom with its enclosed balcony. On the remaining kitchen counter, the family's beloved cat **Didi (דידי זחמ"ל — זכר חתול מחונן לברכה)** famously held court[^blog-einstein].

---

## Family Life, The 1991 Gulf War & Relocation to Oregon

The Einstein apartment provided an idyllic environment for childhood. Guy and Tal could safely run down the stairs unsupervised to play on the expansive lawn between the front and rear buildings or explore the wooded public park. Guy attended the neighborhood Einstein Elementary School, while Tal was enrolled in alternative educational frameworks—the pioneering "Meitar" in Beit Oren and later the Open School.

A searing collective memory etched into the walls of 33A Einstein occurred during the **First Gulf War (January–February 1991)**: the family sat gathered inside the sealed children's room wearing civil defense gas masks, with damp cloths wedged along the door frame, as an Iraqi Scud missile with a conventional explosive warhead struck the Carmel ridge only a few hundred meters away[^blog-einstein].

Between 1994 and 1995, the family relocated to the United States for a 1.5-year sabbatical/assignment in Oregon. Living in a spacious detached suburban house surrounded by Pacific Northwest forests, Tami and Gadi experienced the profound freedom of single-family, ground-attached living—an experience that completely shifted their residential aspirations upon returning to Haifa.

---

## The Birth of the Artist & The Need for Space

By the early 2000s, two converging creative developments signaled that the family had outgrown the apartment:
1. **Tami's Turn to Art:** Preparing for her early retirement from defense engineering, Tami began making art in earnest. The three-bedroom apartment offered no physical space for large-scale painting, plaster casting, or messy tactile experimentation.
2. **Gadi's Woodworking Passion:** Gadi had returned from Oregon deeply inspired by American fine woodworking traditions and needed a dedicated workshop for industrial machines and lumber.

With boys growing into teenagers, parking on Einstein Street becoming congested, and shipping containers of tools and books arriving from abroad, the apartment was literally bursting at the seams. In 2004, after twelve rich years, the family moved into the custom-built home and studio they erected at 80 Greenboim Street in Denya[^blog-einstein].
